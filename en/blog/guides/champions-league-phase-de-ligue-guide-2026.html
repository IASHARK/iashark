Champions League:
how the league phase works

No more groups of four. One table, eight matches per club and places to fight for. Here is how it works, and what to look at before the matches of 3 and 4 November 2026.

How does the league phase work?

Thirty-six clubs play in one single table. Each club plays eight matches: four at home and four away, against eight different opponents. The draw picks two opponents from each of four pots.

A win is worth 3 points, a draw 1 point and a defeat 0. After the eighth matchday, the points are added up.

One detail changes how you read the table: two clubs do not face the same opponents. A table after three matchdays compares different fixture lists.

Who qualifies, and from which position?

Final positionWhat happens
1 to 8Straight into the round of 16.
9 to 24Two-legged play-off to reach the round of 16.
25 to 36Out of the competition.

Finishing in the top eight avoids two extra matches. That is a real scheduling advantage, not just prestige. Among the play-off clubs, those ranked 9 to 16 are seeded and play the second leg at home.

What happens when clubs finish level on points?

Many clubs end up with the same total. They are separated first by goal difference, then by goals scored. Further tie-breakers exist after that.

One goal more or less can therefore cost a place. That is why a club already beaten at half-time keeps attacking: the final margin counts.

What should you check before a league-phase match?

  • What is on the line. A club on 9 points and a club on 3 points are not under the same pressure. Look at who really needs the result.
  • The schedule. Days of rest since the last match, a long trip, a busy weekend in the league: all of it affects rotation.
  • Home or away. Each club plays four times at home. A home match is not read like a trip abroad.
  • Who is missing. An injured or suspended starter changes a match more than a place in the table. Line-ups come out about an hour before kick-off.
  • The odds. They show how the market sees the match. To read them properly, start with the chance hidden in the odds and the bookmaker margin.

How do you read the table along the way?

A partial table is read with three habits.

  • Compare matches played. Two clubs can have the same points after a different number of matches. Check the "played" column.
  • Look at goal difference. It is the first tie-breaker. Two clubs on 6 points with +5 and +1 are not level.
  • Think about the opponents already met. Nine points against three weak sides do not mean the same as nine points against three favourites.

A useful reminder: football is a low-scoring sport. A single goal, one refereeing error or a ball off the post can flip a result. That is why the top of the table does not win every time.

Frequently asked questions

How many matches does a club play in the league phase?

Eight matches against eight different opponents: four at home and four away.

Are there still groups in the Champions League?

No. A single table of 36 clubs replaces the old groups of four.

Are clubs ranked 9th to 24th eliminated?

No. They play a two-legged play-off to reach the round of 16. Only clubs ranked 25th to 36th go out.

When are the matchday four games played?
