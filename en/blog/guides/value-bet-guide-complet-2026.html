Defining the Value Bet

A value bet exists when the estimated probability of an event is higher than the implied probability of the odds offered by the bookmaker.

In other words: if your estimate is correct, the odds pay more than the real risk. Everything rests on that “if”: an estimate can be wrong.

Simple formula: Value = (Estimated probability × Odds) − 1
If the result is positive → value bet (opportunity) | If negative → no value

A Concrete Example

Situation: Arsenal are playing an average side. Suppose a model estimates that Arsenal have a 70% chance of winning, and a bookmaker offers odds of 1.60 on an Arsenal win.

Calculation: (0.70 × 1.60) − 1 = +12% value

This means that, in theory, over a very large number of identical bets, the expected value is +12 for every 100 staked — provided the 70% estimate is correct. Over a few dozen bets, the actual result may be very different.

Implied probability: To convert odds into a probability → 1 ÷ Odds. Example: odds of 1.60 → 1÷1.60 = 62.5% implied probability. If you estimate the probability at 70%, the gap (the edge) is +7.5 points.

The Different Types of Value Bets

Straight Value Bet

The most direct bet: win, draw or loss. It is the most followed market, so the one where the odds already include the most information.

BTTS Value Bet (Both Teams To Score)

Do both teams score? The probability comes from the same score grid as the match result, and a gap is read in the same way.

Over/Under Value Bet

Total goals depends directly on the attacking and defensive strength of both teams. The question is whether the odds already account for it.

Out of Scope: Long-Term Bets

Winner of a league or a competition: IASHARK does not analyse these bets. The model works match by match, on football only.

Stakes — What IASHARK Does Not Say

Once a gap has been spotted, there remains the question of the amount. IASHARK does not suggest any stake. Formulas that size the stake according to the gap assume an exact probability: if it is overestimated, they make you stake too much. The decision to bet, and how much, remains yours.

Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake #1: Confusing a value bet with a sure bet. A bet at odds of 3.00 estimated at 40% loses about 6 times out of 10, and the estimate itself may be wrong.

Mistake #2: Staking too much. Even when the gap looks wide, a big stake exposes you to ruin: losing streaks happen even with correct estimates.

Mistake #3: Only picking heavy favourites. Very short odds pay little, and a single failure wipes out several successes. A high probability is never a certainty.

Value Bet vs Other Strategies

No strategy removes the risk. Doubling the stake after a loss speeds up losses instead of wiping them out. Arbitrage runs into moving odds and account restrictions. Betting at random amounts to paying the operator's margin. Value betting is only a way of reasoning: it is never worth more than the initial estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is a value bet?
A value bet is a bet where the estimated probability of an event is higher than the implied probability of the odds. Formula: Value = (Probability × Odds) − 1. If the result is positive, there is value, provided the estimate is correct.
Can you really make money with value bets?
Only in theory, and with no certainty. If your probabilities are genuinely more accurate than the odds, the expected value is positive over a very large number of bets. In practice, a wrong estimate turns a “value bet” into a losing bet, and even a real gap of a few points can go through long losing streaks.
How can you find value bets yourself?
You need to estimate the probability of each outcome consistently, then compare it with the implied probability of the odds. The hardest part is the estimate: IASHARK calculates it with a statistical model, but it remains an estimate, with limits described on the Methodology page.
How many value bets does IASHARK flag per day?
It varies with the number of matches analysed that day and the odds available. There is no quota: the “value” label only appears from a gap of 3 points, and some days may have none.
What minimum gap is needed for a value bet to be flagged?
The “value” label only appears from a gap of 3 points. Even above that, a gap remains a disagreement between two estimates: it should be read alongside the reliability level and does not increase the probability that the bet comes in.
Do bookmakers close value bettors' accounts?
Yes, some operators restrict the accounts of regular bettors. Only bet with operators licensed in your country: an unlicensed site gives you no protection and no recourse.
