How to choose a licensed bookmaker safely

The checks worth doing before opening any account, wherever you live: authorisation by your own country's regulator, age limits, margins, complaints, self-exclusion and the warning signs of unauthorised sites. No rankings, no operator promotion.

1. Check the operator is authorised in your country

Online betting is regulated country by country, and an authorisation only covers the country that granted it: an operator licensed in one country is not automatically authorised where you live. Check that the operator is authorised by your own country's regulator, using that regulator's official register. Search it for the company name and the exact website address given in the operator's terms and conditions. A logo or a licence number printed on the site is not proof on its own.

Official register
YOUR REGULATOR
Use only the register published by your country's gambling regulator, which you can reach through your government's official website. Check that sports betting is among the activities authorised for that website, and check on the day you do it: registers change.
Foreign licences
NOT ENOUGH
A licence covers the country that issued it. If your country has its own licensing system, only that system counts there; if online betting is not permitted where you live, a foreign licence does not change that.
Lookalike sites and lists
CAUTION
Unofficial lists of "legal bookmakers" can be out of date or commercial. Rely on the regulator's own website and compare the web address letter by letter.

2. 18+ and responsible gambling

Gambling is for adults only. The legal minimum age is set by each country and is 18 or higher depending on where you live: check the rule that applies to you. A licensed operator has to verify your identity and age before you can deposit or withdraw. Before any deposit, check that the site offers:

  • Deposit and loss limits that you set yourself.
  • Time-outs and reality checks showing the time and money spent.
  • A visible link to self-exclusion and to support services.
  • A full account history of bets, deposits and withdrawals.

Only ever use money you can afford to lose. No statistic, model or tip can guarantee a result or any winnings. If gambling is causing you problems, contact the free support services available in your country: your regulator's or health authority's website lists them. See also the IASHARK responsible gambling page.

3. Compare odds and margins

Decimal odds convert into an implied probability: 1 ÷ odds. Add up the implied probabilities of every outcome in a market and the total comes out above 100%. The excess is the operator's margin (also called the overround).

Example with round numbers: a two-way market priced 1.90 and 1.90 gives 52.6% + 52.6% = 105.3%, a margin of about 5%. The lower that total, the less the price costs you. A low margin never makes a bet profitable by itself.

  • Margins differ by operator, league and type of market, and odds move constantly: a fixed ranking is out of date almost immediately.
  • Only compare operators that are authorised in your own country.
  • Read the rules on settlement, void bets, maximum stakes and withdrawals, not just the price.
IASHARK note: the value bet guide explains how to read the probability implied by odds and why the margin matters when comparing it with a statistical estimate.

4. Dispute resolution

Before registering, find the operator's complaints procedure and the independent body it has to refer you to. The usual route is:

  1. Complain to the operator first, in writing, and keep screenshots of every exchange.
  2. Go to the independent dispute resolution body designated by your country's rules if the answer is unsatisfactory or late. Your regulator's website says which body applies and how long you have to wait.
  3. Report illegal practices to your country's regulator.

With an unlicensed operator, these routes are generally not available: your country's regulator has no power over a company outside its supervision.

5. Self-exclusion tools

Besides the limits and account closure each operator offers, many regulated countries run a national self-exclusion scheme that blocks access to all licensed operators at once. Your regulator's website explains whether such a scheme exists where you live and how to register.

Self-exclusion only works with licensed operators: one more reason never to move to an unlicensed site.

6. Warning signs of unlicensed sites

  • No licence information, or only a foreign licence while your country has its own licensing system.
  • The company name or domain is missing from your regulator's register, or differs from the one listed.
  • An account can be opened, or money deposited, without identity and age checks.
  • Pressure: countdowns, "guaranteed" winnings, "sure bets", unsolicited messages on social networks or messaging apps.
  • Advice on getting around restrictions (VPN, accepting players from countries where the site is not licensed, accepting self-excluded people).
  • No accessible complaints procedure, vague terms, or withdrawal conditions that change after a deposit.
  • Unusual payment methods only, such as transfers to private accounts.
Warning: sports betting carries a risk of financial loss and addiction. If gambling stops being a leisure activity, use the self-exclusion tools available in your country and contact a support service. IASHARK does not recommend any operator.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I check whether a bookmaker is authorised where I live?
Search the official register of your own country's gambling regulator for the company name and the exact website address, and check that sports betting is among the authorised activities. A logo or a licence number on the site is not proof.
Is a licence from another country valid where I live?
Not by itself. A licence covers the country that issued it. If your country has its own licensing system, only a licence from that regulator counts there.
Which bookmaker has the best odds?
There is no fixed answer: odds and margins vary by operator, league and market, and change constantly. IASHARK does not rank operators. The margin of a market can be calculated by adding up the implied probabilities (1 divided by the odds) of all its outcomes.
What can I do if a licensed operator refuses to pay out?
Use the operator's complaints procedure first, then the independent dispute resolution body designated in your country. With an unlicensed operator, these remedies are generally not available.
I live in Great Britain or South Africa: where do I check?
In Great Britain, in the UK Gambling Commission public register. In South Africa, in the National Gambling Board list of verified operators, compiled from the provincial licensing authorities.

7. Readers in Great Britain and South Africa

The UK and South African editions of IASHARK link to this English guide. If you live in one of these two countries, these are the official references to use.

Great Britain
UK GAMBLING COMMISSION
  • Licence: gambling businesses serving consumers in Great Britain must be licensed by the UK Gambling Commission. Search the company in the Commission's public register.
  • 18+: the minimum legal age for betting is 18, and online gambling businesses must verify your age and identity before you gamble.
  • GAMSTOP: the free online self-exclusion scheme; every gambling business licensed in Great Britain must participate.
  • Complaints: if the operator has not resolved your complaint after 8 weeks, you can take it to a free, independent ADR provider; the business should be able to tell you which one.
  • Help: National Gambling Helpline, 0808 8020 133, free and available 24/7, and the advice and tools of GambleAware (BeGambleAware).
  • Promotions: IASHARK does not relay gambling offers or incentives. Under the UK advertising rules (CAP Code, section 16), gambling marketing must be socially responsible and must not be directed at under-18s.
PUBLIC REGISTER ↗ GAMSTOP ↗ GAMBLEAWARE ↗
South Africa
NGB · PROVINCIAL BOARDS
  • Licence: gambling licences, including bookmaker licences, are issued by the provincial licensing authorities (provincial gambling boards); the National Gambling Board oversees the regulation of gambling across the country.
  • Verified operators: the National Gambling Board publishes a list of licensed operators compiled from the provincial licensing authorities, with licence number and province, and asks the public not to gamble with operators that are not on it.
  • 18+: under the National Gambling Act 2004, no one under 18 may gamble.
  • Help and self-exclusion: National Responsible Gambling Programme (NRGP), toll-free counselling line 0800 006 008, free and open 24/7, or WhatsApp/SMS on 076 675 0710. The NRGP website also explains self-exclusion.
