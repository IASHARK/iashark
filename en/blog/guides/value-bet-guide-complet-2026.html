Defining the Value Bet

A value bet exists when the true probability of an event is higher than the implied probability of the odds offered by the bookmaker.

In other words, the bookmaker has underestimated the chances of an outcome. You would then hold a mathematical edge over them. It is just like a casino making a mistake in its probabilities — if you spot it, you play with an advantage.

Simple formula: Value = (Estimated probability × Odds) − 1
If the result is positive → value bet (opportunity) | If negative → no value

A Concrete Example

Situation: Arsenal are playing an average side. Our AI estimates that Arsenal have a 70% chance of winning. Betclic offers odds of 1.60 on an Arsenal win.

Calculation: (0.70 × 1.60) − 1 = +12% value

This means that, in theory, over a very large number of identical bets, the expected value is +€12 for every €100 staked — provided the 70% estimate is correct. Over a few dozen bets, the actual result may be very different.

Implied probability: To convert odds into a probability → 1 ÷ Odds. Example: odds of 1.60 → 1÷1.60 = 62.5% implied probability. If you believe the true probability is 70%, you have an edge of +7.5%.

The Different Types of Value Bets

Straight Value Bet

The most direct bet — win, draw or loss. Our AI finds edges on results when bookmakers overestimate or underestimate a team following a misleading recent result.

BTTS Value Bet (Both Teams To Score)

Bookmakers often misjudge BTTS probabilities in mid-table matches. Our AI detects regular edges in this market, particularly in the Premier League and the Bundesliga.

Over/Under Value Bet

Total goals is a market where anomalies are common. A match between two in-form attacks facing two fragile defences can offer an excellent edge on Over 2.5.

Long-Term Value Bet

Bets on the winner of a league or a competition. Bookmakers make more mistakes over the long term because probabilities shift as the season goes on.

Kelly Criterion — The Optimal Stake

Once a value bet has been identified, the question is: how much to stake? The Kelly criterion provides the mathematically optimal answer.

Kelly formula: Stake (%) = (Edge) ÷ (Odds − 1)
Example: Edge +12%, Odds 1.60 → Stake = 0.12 ÷ 0.60 = 20% of the bankroll
In practice: use fractional Kelly (1/4 Kelly) to limit risk = 5% here.

Our AI automatically calculates the optimal stake for each value bet and displays a recommendation of between 1% and 5% of the bankroll.

Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake #1: Confusing a value bet with a safe bet. A value bet at odds of 3.00 can lose 7 times out of 10 and, in theory, still carry a positive expected value over the long term. Consistency matters more than the short term.

Mistake #2: Staking too much. Even with a +20% edge, staking more than 5% of the bankroll exposes you to ruin during a normal losing streak.

Mistake #3: Only picking heavy favourites. Very short odds (1.10-1.25) rarely offer value — bookmakers are accurate in these markets.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is a value bet?
A value bet is a bet where the true probability of an event is higher than the implied probability of the odds. Formula: Value = (Probability × Odds) − 1. If the result is positive, there is value.
Can you really make money with value bets?
Only in theory, and with no guarantee. If your probabilities are genuinely more accurate than the odds, the expected value is positive over a sufficiently large sample of bets (500+). In practice, an incorrect estimate turns a “value bet” into a losing bet, and even a genuine 5% edge can go through long losing streaks.
How can you find value bets yourself?
You need to estimate the true probability of each outcome and compare it with the odds. This is hard to do manually across 200+ variables — which is why our IASHARK AI automates the process.
How many value bets does the IASHARK AI detect per day?
It varies depending on the number of matches analysed that day and the quality of the data available (line-ups, odds) — there is no fixed quota and no guaranteed number.
What minimum edge makes a value bet worthwhile?
IASHARK displays the edge as soon as it is positive, with no hidden arbitrary threshold. The higher the edge, the wider the gap between the model and the market — but a small edge is still a small edge, to be weighed against the reliability level and the sample size.
Do bookmakers close value bettors' accounts?
Traditional bookmakers may limit or even close the accounts of consistently winning bettors. Pinnacle and crypto bookmakers do not apply this practice — which is why diversifying your accounts is considered essential.
