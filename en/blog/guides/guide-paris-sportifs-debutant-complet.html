Rule #1 — The Bankroll

Before placing a single bet, set your bankroll — the total budget you are willing to devote to betting. It is money you accept you could lose entirely without it affecting your life.

The golden rule: Never stake more than 2 to 5% of your bankroll per bet.
Bankroll of €200 → maximum stake of €4 to €10 per bet.
This rule protects you against the inevitable losing streaks, even with a good strategy.

The 4 Types of Bets

1. Single Bet — The Classic

You bet on a single outcome. Clear, simple and recommended for beginners. Example: PSG to win, Over 2.5 goals, BTTS Yes.

2. Accumulator — Risky

You combine several selections. The odds multiply, but every result has to be correct. A 5-selection accumulator with a 65% success rate on each selection has only an 11% chance of coming in. Our AI rarely recommends accumulators of more than 3 selections.

3. In-Play Betting — Live

You bet while the match is in progress. Odds change in real time. It requires sound tactical knowledge and excellent emotional control.

4. Long-Term Bets

League winner, top scorer of the season, relegated team. High odds, but your money is tied up for a long time.

The 7 Mistakes Beginners Make

1. Betting with your heart — Never bet on your favourite team or against your rival. Emotion clouds judgement.

2. Chasing losses — Doubling your stake after a loss to "win it back" is a guaranteed route to ruin.

3. Ignoring the odds — Even the best prediction can be a bad deal if the odds are too low.

4. Too many accumulators — Accumulators make bookmakers rich, not bettors.

5. Betting without a strategy — Betting on instinct without analysis = losing in the long run.

6. Neglecting your bankroll — Staking too much exposes you to ruin, even with a good strategy.

7. Using only one bookmaker — Having several accounts means you can always take the best odds available.

A Methodical Approach

A methodical approach rests on two pillars: looking for value bets (odds higher than the estimated probability justifies) and applying strict bankroll management. Even then, no result is guaranteed: variance can produce long losing streaks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you really make money from sports betting?
Nothing guarantees it. Fewer than 5% of bettors win over the long term, and the bookmaker's margin works against everyone else. Value bets and strict bankroll management limit mistakes, but they remove neither the risk nor the variance. IASHARK provides probabilities and a comparison with the odds, never a guarantee of winnings.
What budget do you need to start sports betting?
Keep your starting budget to €50 to €100 — money you are prepared to lose. At 2% of your bankroll per bet, that gives you 50 bets to learn without significant risk. Only increase your bankroll once you have demonstrated positive profitability.
Which bets are the easiest for beginners?
Singles on big favourites playing at home are the most predictable. But they are not necessarily the most profitable: the value bets identified by our AI are a more relevant benchmark — any positive edge counts, and there is no arbitrary threshold.
Can a beginner use IASHARK?
Absolutely — IASHARK is designed to be used at every level. The interface is simple: you see the prediction, the probability, the reliability, the edge and the suggested stake. There is no need to understand advanced statistics.
How long before you know whether a strategy is profitable?
You need at least 100 to 200 bets for a statistically meaningful assessment. Over 20-30 bets, results are too heavily influenced by luck. Be patient and keep a log of your performance.
