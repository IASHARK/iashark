Update frequency

  • The calculation runs automatically once a day at 06:00 UTC (08:00 in South Africa). Publication follows once the calculation has finished.
  • Each run covers today's, tomorrow's and the following day's matches, and recalculates everything. The same match can therefore be analysed up to three times: the latest version replaces the previous one.
  • A match that has already started, is about to start when the calculation runs, or is no longer listed as upcoming (postponed, cancelled, in play, finished) gets no bet and no analysis. It may still be shown as a fact sheet.
  • No automatic recalculation is scheduled in the hours before kick-off. Separate jobs regularly record how odds move and the closing odds: they are used for monitoring and do not change published analyses.

3. How the model calculates

In plain terms:

  1. Estimate each team's expected goals. The system starts from the goals scored and conceded by the home team when playing at home, and by the away team when playing away. It compares them with the competition average.
  2. Avoid jumping to conclusions early in the season. When few matches have been played, the current season is blended with the previous season and with a reference average. The previous season counts for less as more matches are played.
  3. Adjust for chance quality. When enough recent matches are available, expected goals are adjusted using recent xG and shooting accuracy.
  4. Work out the probability of every score. From the two expected-goals figures, the model calculates the probability of each score (0-0, 1-0, 2-1 and so on). It then derives the probability of each market: 1X2 result, double chance, total goals, both teams to score, team goals, first half, and more.
  5. Correct overconfidence. On past seasons, the raw model was too confident about high probabilities and not confident enough about low ones. A correction learnt from that history is applied to the 1X2, over/under 2.5 goals and both teams to score markets.
  6. Simulate the match. The match is simulated 5,000 times to show the most likely scores.

Reading a probability: 60% means that, over a large number of comparable matches, the outcome should happen about 6 times in 10, and so not happen about 4 times in 10. This assumes a well-calibrated model, which still has to be measured in real conditions.

The model's probability is calculated without ever using odds. Odds are only used afterwards, to compare the model's estimate with the market's.

Technical detail

  • Expected goals: goals for and against of the home team (at home) and of the away team (away), smoothed early in the season with the previous season and a league prior, then bounded to avoid extreme values.
  • Adjustments: recent expected goals (xG) and a shooting accuracy factor, only when each team has enough recent matches.
  • Score matrix: Dixon-Coles model with adaptive truncation of the number of goals.
  • Calibration: isotonic regression applied to 1X2, over 2.5 goals and both teams to score: yes, fitted on past seasons and checked on seasons that were not used for fitting. Other markets are not calibrated.
  • Monte Carlo: 5,000 simulations with a seed derived from expected goals, so results are reproducible. They are used to display the most likely scores, filtered to stay consistent with the selected market. They are not used to choose the bet.
  • Market probability: Pinnacle odds when the 1X2 set is complete, otherwise odds collected via API-Football. The bookmaker margin is removed with Shin's method for 1X2 and for the over/under 2.5 goals and both-teams-to-score yes/no pairs. For other markets, the probability is 1 ÷ odds, margin included.
  • Gap: model probability minus market probability, in percentage points.
  • Elo: displayed, but not used in the probabilities.
  • Model version: score-matrix-dc-early-season-v2.

4. How a bet is selected, or not

The rule. Among the markets that the model calculates and that a bookmaker actually prices, the system selects the one with the highest estimated probability, with two safeguards:

  • a minimum price;
  • a probability cap: above it, the probability and the odds cannot describe the same event, so the market is discarded.

The selected market is therefore the most likely one according to the model, not the one with the largest gap to the market. The gap is calculated afterwards and shown separately.

When no market reaches the minimum price:

  • if markets are priced but all below the minimum, the most likely one is still selected and flagged as below the threshold;
  • if no odds are available but the model could still calculate, its most likely market is published without odds and without a gap.

Risk level shown. It depends only on the price of the selected market, in three bands (low, moderate, high): the higher the price, the higher the level shown.

Why some matches show "no bet selected":

  • The match has started or is about to start when the calculation runs, or is no longer listed as upcoming: no prediction is produced after kick-off.
  • Current-season data unavailable because the provider refused or failed the request (quota, error). The system abstains rather than rely on data whose state it does not know.
  • Not enough history: the home team has played no home match, or the away team no away match, in the competition this season or last season.

If the provider refuses requests during a run and a valid analysis already existed for the match, the previous analysis is kept and labelled as coming from the previous run, unless the match has started in the meantime.

Stricter selection. In competitions where the score model has passed a dedicated validation (currently the Premier League only), a stricter selection can replace the selected market on one match per run. It requires a minimum price, a minimum probability, a clear lead over the second outcome of the same market and a limited difference from the market probability. Candidates are then ranked by a robustness score. This mechanism is in an observation phase: its validation on betting results is not finished.

How the free analysis of the day is chosen. One analysis is offered for each of the next two match days that have analyses (in practice, today and tomorrow). The system picks it from matches whose selected market has real odds, taking the one with the highest estimated value (model probability × odds − 1), preferring probabilities above a floor when any exist. This value is a model calculation, not a promise of any outcome.

5. Reading the estimated probability and reliability

Estimated probability (out of 10). This is the model probability for the selected market, divided by 10: 64% is shown as 6.4/10. It is not an independent measure and does not reflect data quality. It is only shown when a market is selected, on the free analysis of the day and to subscribers.

Reliability (High, Medium, Low). It is calculated separately from the probability, from three measurable signals, each rated on three levels and then combined:

SignalWhat is measured
Agreement between calculationsSpread of the home-win probability across three calculations: Poisson, Dixon-Coles and simulation
Data qualityAvailability of both teams' statistics, odds, injuries, head-to-head records, Elo and line-ups (score out of 100)
Sample sizeMatches played this season by whichever team has played fewer

How to use it:

  • High reliability means the calculation was made in good conditions. It does not increase the chance of the bet landing.
  • The three calculations in the agreement signal share the same expected goals. "Strong" agreement is therefore common and is not independent confirmation.
  • An empty injury list counts as missing data. Official line-ups are rarely known when the daily calculation runs.
  • Reliability does not include how well the model is calibrated in real conditions.

6. Limits of the model

  • Late news. The calculation runs once a day. An injury in the warm-up, rotation, a change of manager or an unexpected line-up after that point is not taken into account. Known absences are displayed but do not change the probabilities.
  • Early season and small samples. With few matches, the model relies more on the previous season and on a reference average. A promoted team has no history in the competition. Transfers are not built into team strength.
  • Less-covered competitions. Some data is missing depending on the competition (injuries, Elo, Pinnacle odds), which lowers the data quality score. So far, only the Premier League has passed the dedicated validation of the score model. Elsewhere, the same generic model is applied without competition-specific validation.
  • What the model ignores. What is at stake, derbies, fatigue, weather, the referee and absences are shown as context. They are not used to calculate probabilities.
  • Partial calibration. Only three markets are corrected by calibration, and that correction was learnt on a limited number of leagues.
  • Variance. Football produces few goals and a lot of randomness. An outcome estimated at 70% does not happen about 3 times in 10. Losing runs happen even when estimates are sound. Never bet money you need.
  • AI-written texts. The commentary can contain mistakes of interpretation. Where they differ, the calculated figures take precedence.

7. The role of AI and human oversight

What is automated, with no human involvement match by match:

  • data collection and calculations;
  • choice of the selected market and of the free analysis of the day;
  • writing of the analysis texts by a language model (Claude Sonnet, Anthropic), from figures that have already been calculated;
  • translation of those texts by another model (Claude Haiku) into English, Spanish, Mexican Spanish, German, Italian and Portuguese;
  • publication on the website.

What the AI does not do. The language model does not calculate, choose or change any probability, odds, selected market or risk level, nor the choice of likely scorers. These are fixed by the calculation before the model is involved, and it is instructed to cite only the data it is given.

  • If its response cannot be used, the analysis is published without text and the selection stays the same.
  • If a translation is invalid, the text is hidden rather than shown in another language.

What is not checked automatically: whether every figure quoted in a text matches the data. No analysis is reviewed by a person before publication.

What depends on human decisions:

  • which competitions are covered;
  • the design of the model and its thresholds, and any change to them;
  • fitting and validating the calibration offline;
  • code testing;
  • monitoring: automated checks block publication if public files are out of date or empty, and alert the team to incidents.

9. Last updated and frequently asked questions

Last updated: 14 September 2026. This page describes how the system worked on that date.

Does the AI choose the bets?

No. The selected market is chosen by a fixed calculation rule. The language model only writes the accompanying text and cannot change any figure.

Why is no bet selected on some matches?

Because the match had already started or was about to start when the calculation ran, or because the model could not produce an honest calculation: not enough history, or current-season data unavailable. The system prefers to abstain.

Does "High" reliability mean the bet will land?

No. It means the data was complete and the calculation consistent. A probability is still a probability: a 65% outcome fails to happen about one time in three.

Does the analysis change if a player is injured on match day?

No. The calculation runs once a day and is not repeated before kick-off. Always check the official line-ups.
