1. One league, several names

South Africa's top flight is run by the Premier Soccer League (PSL), founded in 1996. Supporters still simply call it "the PSL", but the competition's official name has always followed its sponsor. Over the years it has been sponsored by Castle Lager, then ABSA, then DStv and, since 2024, by the title sponsor that gives the competition its current name: the Betway Premiership.

The league's commercial name includes a betting brand. Using it here simply reflects the competition's official title: IASHARK has no link with any operator and does not recommend one.

2. The format: 16 clubs, 30 rounds

The Premiership has 16 clubs. Each plays every other club twice, once at home and once away, for a total of 30 league matches. The season usually runs from August to May, broadly in step with the European calendar rather than the calendar year.

With only 30 rounds, every result weighs more in the final log than it would in a 38-match league. It also means that season statistics rest on smaller samples. A run of three or four results can transform how a team looks on paper, which is worth remembering before drawing strong conclusions from a few weeks of form.

The calendar also includes international breaks and, for some clubs, continental fixtures, so the gap between league rounds is not always regular. Clubs can go through dense spells with several matches in a short period, followed by quieter weeks, and that rhythm is part of the context of every fixture.

3. Relegation and the play-off

The club finishing bottom of the log is relegated automatically to the second tier, the Motsepe Foundation Championship. The club finishing 15th is not safe either: it enters a promotion/relegation play-off against challengers from the Championship, played on a home-and-away basis, and only the side that tops that mini-league plays in the Premiership the following season.

For a team in 15th place, the season therefore extends beyond the final league round. That detail matters when you look at late-season fixtures involving clubs near the bottom, because avoiding the last two places and avoiding the play-off are two different objectives.

4. Places in African competitions

South African clubs compete in the two CAF club competitions. Under the current allocation, the Premiership champions and runners-up enter the CAF Champions League, while the third-placed club and the Nedbank Cup winners enter the CAF Confederation Cup.

Continental football brings long journeys across the continent and extra midweek fixtures. For clubs involved, league matches either side of a CAF tie can be affected by travel and rotation, so it pays to check the continental schedule as well as the domestic fixture list. South African clubs have a real pedigree here: Orlando Pirates won the African Cup of Champions Clubs in 1995.

5. A season with several trophies

The league is only part of the story. The MTN8 brings together the top eight clubs from the previous season's log, the Nedbank Cup is the main knockout competition, and the Carling Knockout completes the cup calendar. Cup ties change squad selection and fixture congestion for the clubs that go deep in them. We explain how each works in our guide to the PSL cup competitions.

6. The clubs that shape the title race

Mamelodi Sundowns have been the dominant force of the recent era, winning the league title every season from 2017-18 to 2024-25. Kaizer Chiefs and Orlando Pirates, the two Soweto giants, remain the most widely supported clubs in the country, and their meetings in the Soweto Derby are among the biggest fixtures in African football.

7. Reading PSL matches with data

The principles are the same as anywhere else, but a few points deserve extra attention in South African football:

  1. Log position versus underlying performance. Over 30 rounds, luck in close matches can move a team several places. Goals scored and conceded over a longer period, and chance quality where it is available, are steadier guides than the current position.
  2. Fixture congestion. Clubs combining league, domestic cups and CAF football play more often. Check how many days separate a team's matches, not just who they are playing.
  3. Travel and geography. Clubs are spread from the Highveld around Johannesburg and Pretoria, which sits at high altitude, to coastal cities such as Durban and Cape Town. Distances and conditions are part of the context of every round; whether they change a specific result is a question for data rather than assumption.
  4. Team news. Squad information can arrive late. Confirmed line-ups are more reliable than pre-match speculation, especially when a club is rotating between competitions.
  5. Data depth. Detailed historical statistics are thinner for the PSL than for the major European leagues. Any estimate built on them should come with a wider margin of uncertainty.
